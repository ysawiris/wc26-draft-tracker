    How It Works

    The draft, in four steps

    1. 1
      The draw. Each team was randomly handed a World Cup group, A–L. Ten teams, twelve groups — so two groups (A and I) go unclaimed.
    2. 2
      Goals decide it. Every goal scored by the four nations in your group counts, across the whole group stage. Most goals takes the No. 1 pick.
    3. 3
      Tiebreaker: cards. Level on goals? More cards in your group wins the tie — yellow = +1, red = +2.
    4. 4
      Final order. The ranking sets the official draft order, picks 1 through 10.

    World Cup 2026 — a new format

    The biggest World Cup ever: 48 teams (up from 32) and, for the first time, three hosts — the United States, Mexico and Canada — 16 host cities, 104 matches where the old format had 64. It opened June 11 at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City, where Mexico beat South Africa 2–0.

    The draw: 12 groups, A–L, four nations each. Every group plays 6 matches — 72 group-stage matches from June 11 to June 27. (Group standings tiebreak on points, goal difference, goals scored, head-to-head, then fair play — FIFA's problem, not ours.)

    Then a brand-new knockout round: the Round of 32. The top two from every group advance, plus the 8 best third-placed teams — 32 of the 48 go through. From there it's R32 → R16 → quarterfinals → semifinals, and the final on July 19 at MetLife Stadium, New York / New Jersey.

    For the league, only the group stage counts — the 72 group matches and nothing else. The draft order locks at the final group-stage whistle on June 27. The knockouts are just for watching.
